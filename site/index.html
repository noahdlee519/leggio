    Prefacevii

    You learn a language by reading it.

    When you read a lot in a language, the same words keep turning up in different sentences, and one day you find you already know them. Getting there is slow, though. Every few lines there’s a word you don’t know, so you look it up in another tab and then hunt for your place again. After a few pages of that, it’s tempting to read a translation instead.

    Leggio keeps you in the original. When a word stops you, its meaning appears right beside it, like the facing page of a bilingual edition, and the page you’re reading never changes. Save the words that matter to you, then carry on with the next line.

    A leggio is the stand that holds what you’re reading. That’s the whole job.

    Notes on useviii

    How to read with Leggio

    1. Open something worth reading.

      A news article, a paper, a novel, a PDF from class. Leggio works on web pages and on PDFs in Chrome.

    2. Select what stops you.

      The translation appears next to the words you selected, and the page stays in the original. You read on without switching tabs.

    3. Keep the words worth keeping.

      Save a word and it goes to your list, stored in your browser, ready when you come back to it.

    Your reading stays on your computer.

    Leggio translates with the models built into Chrome, so the words you select are translated on your own machine. The words you save are kept in your browser. There’s no account to make, no server of ours in between, and nothing tracking what you read.

    What the columns mean

    Price
    What it costs to use. Where there’s a paid plan, the lowest monthly price on the maker’s own pricing page or app store listing; prices vary by country.
    On your device
    The words you look up are translated on your computer, not sent to a server.
    No account
    You can look a word up without signing up or signing in.
    Your own API key
    You can connect your own key for a translation or AI service, and choose which one translates.
    One key or click
    Select a word on the page, and one key or click shows its translation right there, without another tab.
    Meaning in context
    It tells you what the word means in your sentence, not just its translation on its own or its dictionary entries.
    Idioms
    It notices when the word you look up belongs to an idiom, and gives the idiom’s meaning instead of a word-for-word translation.
    Read aloud
    It can say the word or the sentence.
    PDFs, even scanned
    It works on PDFs as you read them, including scanned pages that are only pictures of text.
    Word list and export
    It keeps the words you look up for later, and lets you take them elsewhere.
