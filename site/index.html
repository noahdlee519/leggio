    You learn a language by reading it.

    Drills teach you the rules. Reading teaches you the language: how words sit together, which ones keep coming back, what a sentence sounds like when someone meant it. The trouble is that reading in a language you’re still learning is slow, and the moment you open a translation, you stop learning.

    Leggio keeps you in the original. When a word stops you, its meaning appears right beside it, like the facing page of a bilingual edition, and the page you’re reading never changes. Save the words that matter to you, then carry on with the next line.

    A leggio is the stand that holds what you’re reading. That’s the whole job.

    How to read with Leggio

    1. Open something worth reading.

      A news article, a paper, a novel, a PDF from class. Leggio works on web pages and on PDFs in Chrome.

    2. Select what stops you.

      The translation appears next to the words you selected, and the page stays in the original. You read on without switching tabs.

    3. Keep the words worth keeping.

      Save a word and it goes to your list, stored in your browser, ready when you come back to it.

    Your reading stays on your computer.

    Leggio translates with the models built into Chrome, so the words you select are translated on your own machine. The words you save are kept in your browser. There’s no account to make, no server of ours in between, and nothing tracking what you read.
