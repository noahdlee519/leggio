The short version

  • By default, Leggio translates on your device, with the translator built into Chrome. The text you look up isn’t sent to us or to anyone else.
  • If you add your own API key for DeepL or Google Cloud Translation and choose that engine, the words you look up and their sentences are sent to that service to be translated.
  • Your word list stays on your computer. Your settings sync through your Google account if you use Chrome sync.
  • The Leggio extension has no accounts, no servers and no analytics. We never receive your data, and we don’t sell or share it.
  • This website counts visits with anonymous, cookie-free analytics. See This website.

Translation

When you select a word or phrase and press Leggio’s translate or save key, Leggio translates it together with the sentence it’s in, so it can show what the word means in that sentence.

By default, Leggio uses Chrome’s built-in Translator. Chrome runs the translation on your own computer, so the text isn’t sent to any server, ours or anyone else’s. The first time you translate between a pair of languages, Chrome downloads a translation model. Chrome handles that download itself, and the text you’re reading isn’t part of it. Chrome’s own data practices are covered by Google’s privacy policy.

To tell which language a page is in, Leggio uses Chrome’s built-in Language Detector, or a small detector that comes with the extension. Both run on your computer.

Optional engines. In Leggio’s settings you can add your own API key for DeepL or Google Cloud Translation and choose that engine instead. Then the words you look up or save, and the sentences they’re in, are sent to that service under your account to be translated. That provider’s terms and privacy policy apply (DeepL, Google), and it may bill your account for the characters translated. Your API keys are stored only on your computer, aren’t synced, and are only ever sent to their own service.

On web pages

So that its keys work on the pages you read, Leggio adds a small script to web pages. It only listens for your translate and save keys. It doesn’t read or record the page, and does nothing else until you press one of them with text selected.

When you do, Leggio reads the text you selected and the sentence around it, and translates them as described above. Nothing else on the page is read. You can turn this off in Leggio’s settings, under “Use Leggio on web pages”.

PDFs and scanned pages

When you open a PDF, Leggio shows it in its own reader, inside your browser. For a PDF on a website, Leggio downloads it from that website, as Chrome’s own viewer would, including PDFs that need you to be signed in. For a PDF on your computer, Leggio reads the file where it is. Leggio doesn’t upload your PDFs anywhere.

Text recognition (OCR) for scanned pages runs on your computer. The first time it reads a language, Leggio downloads that language’s recognition data, a few megabytes, from cdn.jsdelivr.net. Nothing from your document is sent in that request.

In Chrome’s own PDF viewer, Leggio can’t see what you select, so you copy the text and press Leggio’s shortcut instead. Leggio then reads the clipboard once, to translate what you copied. It reads the clipboard only when you press that shortcut.

Listening to a word

When you press the speaker button, Chrome reads the word or sentence aloud. Depending on the language and your computer, Chrome uses a voice installed on your computer or one of Google’s online voices. With an online voice, Chrome sends that text to Google to be spoken, and Google’s privacy policy applies.

What Leggio keeps on your computer

  • Your settings, such as your languages, keys and options, in Chrome’s extension storage. If you use Chrome sync, Chrome syncs them to your other computers through your Google account.
  • Your word list: each word or phrase you save, its meaning, the sentence it came from and that sentence’s translation, when you saved it, and where you found it: the PDF’s name, address and page, or the web page’s title and address. It stays on this computer and isn’t synced.
  • API keys for DeepL or Google Cloud, if you add any. They stay on this computer.
  • Text recognized on scanned pages, so a scanned document opens quickly the next time.
  • Which open tabs are showing a PDF, so Leggio can open it in its reader. This is cleared when you close Chrome.

None of this is sent to us, and we can’t see it.

What Leggio doesn’t do

  • It doesn’t send anything to us. There are no Leggio servers and no accounts.
  • It doesn’t collect analytics, usage statistics, crash reports or any other telemetry.
  • It doesn’t record your browsing history. It looks at the pages you open only to tell whether one is a PDF.
  • It doesn’t show ads or use advertising identifiers.

Permissions

Chrome lists Leggio’s permissions when you install it. This is what each one is for:

  • Read and change all your data on all websites: so the translate and save keys work on the pages you read (see On web pages), to download a PDF you open into Leggio’s reader, and, if you add a key, to reach DeepL or Google Cloud.
  • Web requests and navigation: to notice when a tab is opening a PDF, so it opens in Leggio’s reader. Leggio doesn’t block or change any request, and keeps no record of them.
  • Clipboard: read only when you press Leggio’s shortcut in Chrome’s own PDF viewer.
  • Storage: for your settings and word list, as described above.
  • Right-click menu: adds “Read with Leggio” to links.

Sharing and selling

We don’t sell, rent or share your data, and we don’t use it for advertising, for creditworthiness or for any purpose unrelated to Leggio’s translation features. Leggio never sends your data to us, so we have nothing to share. Text only goes to the services described above, and only to do what you asked: the translation engine you added a key for, and Google when Chrome reads a word aloud with an online voice.

Leggio’s use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Deleting your data

You can delete saved words one at a time, or all the words from one PDF or web page, and you can export your list as a CSV file first if you want to keep it. Removing Leggio from Chrome (at chrome://extensions) deletes everything it stored on your computer.

Children

Leggio is a general-audience tool. It doesn’t collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

This website

This website doesn’t use cookies or ads, and it loads everything, fonts included, from its own domain. The demo on the home page runs entirely in your browser and doesn’t save anything.

To see how many people visit and which pages they read, the website uses Vercel Web Analytics, from our hosting provider, Vercel. When you open a page, it records the page you viewed, the site that linked you here, your country or region, and your browser, operating system and device type. It doesn’t use cookies, and what it records isn’t tied to you or to your IP address. To count visitors, it uses an anonymous identifier that resets every day, so your visits can’t be linked from one day to the next or to other websites. We only look at the totals. This applies to the website only. The Leggio extension has no analytics.

Our hosting provider may also keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses and request times, to keep the site secure and running.

Changes to this policy

If we change how Leggio handles data, we’ll update this page and the effective date at the top before the change takes effect.
